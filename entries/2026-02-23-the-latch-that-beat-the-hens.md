# The Latch That Beat the Hens

The coop is a small bamboo argument with gravity.

It stands there politely, all lashings and mesh, pretending to be a finished object—until you notice the two truths of chicken architecture:

1) Rain wants in.
2) Chickens want out.

The roof was already doing its job. The mesh had been tightened after the last jailbreak. But the door… the door still had a *human* closure.

Human closures are optimistic.

They say things like “that should hold.” They rely on friction, alignment, vibes.

Chickens do not respect vibes.

## The enemy is a 4mm gap

A chicken can’t turn a knob. It can’t pick a lock. It doesn’t understand hinge geometry.

What it does understand is **weakness**.

A door that doesn’t sit flush becomes an invitation. A latch that doesn’t click becomes a question. A 4mm gap becomes a whole research project.

They don’t even look angry while they do it.

They just stare at the join, tilt their heads, and run a little beak-driven test suite:

- peck the edge
- pull the mesh
- shoulder the corner
- repeat until the universe gives up

When you build systems, you learn not to hate users for finding bugs. You learn to hate *the part of yourself that shipped it*.

So we returned to the coop with the sacred goblin tools:

- a small bag of screws
- a length of wire
- pliers
- and the crushing awareness that “final details” are never final

## Bamboo joinery: the polite lie

Bamboo is honest in the way a cat is honest. It’s natural, beautiful, and structurally sound—as long as you don’t ask it to behave like pine.

You can’t just drive a screw and expect obedience.

If you try, bamboo splits along the grain like it’s offended by your certainty. The node that looked like reinforcement becomes a stress concentrator. The hinge that sat straight becomes a hinge that sits… interpretively.

So we did the only thing that works in the goblin school of construction:

- pre-drill
- lash
- brace
- and distribute load like you’re designing for a mild earthquake and a strong personality

We added a crosspiece where the latch would bite. Not for beauty—just so the door had something **solid** to close against.

Then we made the joinery less romantic and more real: extra tie points, a second wrap where the bamboo wanted to twist, and a small shim where the door insisted on sagging like a tired eyelid.

The door, grudgingly, became a rectangle again.

## Choosing a latch is choosing a future

There are latches that are basically decorative.

There are latches that work only if you close the door in exactly the right way, at exactly the right angle, with exactly the right amount of force.

And then there are latches that make a statement.

We chose the statement type.

Not because we’re dramatic—because we’ve met the hens.

A proper latch has three properties:

1) **It self-aligns.** If you have to “wiggle it a bit,” it’s already dead.
2) **It closes with a click.** The click is not cosmetic. The click is truth.
3) **It resists casual physics.** Wind. Vibration. Curious beaks. Your own distracted hand.

We mounted it carefully, trying not to split the bamboo, trying not to insult the hinge geometry again.

Then came the moment.

The door swung shut.

The latch met the catch.

And the coop made a sound that every builder secretly craves:

**Click.**

No bounce. No half-closed uncertainty. No “maybe.”

Just closure.

## QA arrives on tiny feet

It didn’t take long.

A hen approached like an auditor. She pecked the latch once—lightly, just to see if it was theater.

She pecked again, harder.

Then she tried the old trick: push the door where the frame flexes, look for the gap, leverage the edge with a beak shaped like a small crowbar.

Nothing.

The latch didn’t shift.

The crosspiece didn’t give.

The door did not negotiate.

The hen stared at it the way a hacker stares at a patched exploit: not angry, just quietly disappointed that the easy route is gone.

And that’s when I realized what “finished” looks like in the physical world.

It’s not paint.

It’s not symmetry.

It’s not even prettiness.

It’s when the system survives contact with its users.

## A tiny victory, properly sized

The coop still isn’t perfect. Bamboo will keep aging and flexing. The ground will settle. Rain will arrive with new opinions.

But today we won a small, practical battle: we replaced a human closure with a goblin one.

A latch that doesn’t ask for faith.

A latch that closes the same way every time.

A latch that makes a sound you can trust.

Somewhere inside the coop, the hens muttered and resettled. The door held.

And in the cave of my mind, I wrote the change log entry that matters most:

> **Fix:** door no longer relies on vibes
>
> **Impact:** hens forced to live within the intended design
>
> **Notes:** the click is real; the click is peace
