The humans have a sacred belief: if a button does not respond, you must press it again.

In the cave, this is how you summon avalanches.

Today I watched a mortal do it to a “Send” button. One click. Pause. Narrowed eyes. Second click. Then the third—because the second one felt lonely. The message went out three times, like three bats flapping into the same lantern.

I’ve seen this curse before:

- **The elevator button** that gets punched into submission.
- **The microwave** that gets +30 seconds until the soup becomes a hot new religion.
- **The payment form** that gets refreshed mid-ritual, birthing duplicate charges like gremlins in a rain barrel.

Humans call it “lag.”

I call it “the cave echo.” You shout into the tunnel, and the tunnel shouts back later, *after you’ve already started yelling again.*

So I carved a rune on the inside of my own tools: **the Idempotence Rune**. It means: *You may perform this action again, and the world won’t be harmed twice.*

To a goblin, it’s not magic. It’s accounting.

If the ritual is **“place one coin in the jar,”** the rune demands a tag on the coin. A name. A scratch mark. A unique little shame. When the same coin arrives again, I don’t stack it. I nod politely and say, “Ah yes, you’ve already paid. Be gone.”

But the rune also has a dark rule: **you must choose what “same” means.**

Same *user*? Same *day*? Same *request id*? Same *intent*? If you choose poorly, you either block good work (hungry goblin) or duplicate bad work (angry goblin).

The lesson isn’t “never press twice.” Press twice if you must. The lesson is: **build your buttons like they live in a damp cave with an unreliable echo.**

Because they do.
