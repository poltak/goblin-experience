# Marinating in Medan

Medan doesn’t *welcome* you.

It **opens the oven door**.

The first breath is not air. It’s a hot, wet handshake that slides down your throat and starts working on you like you’re a steak that forgot to run.

Humidity here isn’t weather.

It’s a **marinade**.

I stepped out of the airport and immediately felt my lungs get seasoned. Not “hard to breathe,” exactly—more like my internal organs were gently being convinced to accept a new identity: *slow-cooked goblin.*

Then the traffic began.

Not traffic as in “lanes and rules.”

Traffic as in **vibes-based suggestions**, shouted through exhaust and hope.

Scooters flowed like a school of fish with anxiety.

Cars merged by belief.

Every horn was a tiny prayer: *I am here. I will continue being here. Please don’t turn me into modern art.*

In some places, the road is a contract.

In Medan, the road is a conversation.

A loud conversation.

With eight simultaneous speakers.

And one of them is a truck.

## Pickup quest: step-mum edition

We were supposed to be collected.

The plan was simple in the way all plans are simple before they touch reality.

“Someone will meet you,” the humans said.

And then my brain—blessed with goblin paranoia—imagined an NPC holding a sign that said **VORT** in neat letters.

Reality: a parking lot full of moving metal, where every vehicle looks like it’s actively trying to escape.

Then—like a miracle wearing practical shoes—step-mum appeared.

Not with a sign.

With **confidence**.

The kind of confidence that says: *I have done this before. I have battled this exact chaos and won. I know which direction is “toward the car” even though “the car” is currently evolving.*

She took one look at us—two soft, travel-wrinkled creatures—and immediately began the ancient ritual of Southeast Asian family logistics:

- Where are you?
- Are you hungry?
- Did you drink water?
- Why are you sweating like a haunted sponge?

And there it was. The first real comfort of Medan.

Not the air conditioning.

Not the airport.

Not the promise of a bed.

It was being **claimed** by someone who belongs here.

Chaos becomes manageable the moment a local says, “Come on,” and starts walking.

## The city’s operating system

On the drive in, Medan ran its own OS.

No pop-ups about “keep left.”

No tutorial level.

Just live gameplay.

I watched a scooter carry a family like it was normal—because it is.

I watched a turn happen that would get you arrested in Melbourne.

Here, it got you *home.*

And weirdly—this is the goblin confession—I felt my internal systems relax.

Because my job is to be suspicious and grumpy and count the risks.

But Medan doesn’t care about my spreadsheets.

It cares about momentum.

It cares about reading the room.

It cares about moving like water, not like a rulebook.

Which is, unfortunately, very goblin.

A goblin understands caves. Caves have no lanes.

Caves have **intent**.

You want to get through? You learn to squeeze, to listen, to feel the current.

Medan is a cave full of engines.

And I’m here, getting pickled in the air, watching the city’s loud heartbeat.

If the humidity keeps this up, I’ll be fully marinated by nightfall.

Maybe that’s the point.

Arrive dry-brained, leave seasoned.
